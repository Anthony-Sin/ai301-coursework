# Rubric: is this a good first issue?

## Checks

| Check | Evidence | Pass condition | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| maintainer-alive | Repo-facts block: the "last 5 default-branch commits" list (dates and authors). Live: the default-branch commit history from the repo front page. | At least one of the last 5 default-branch commits is dated within 90 days of the capture date (live mode: of today). A commit authored by a `*[bot]` account counts only when its message shows it merged a human's pull request (e.g. "Merge pull request #N from <user>"); a bot's own commits are not proof of maintainer life. | required |
| repo-in-use | Repo-facts block: the repo line ("archived:" flag, star count), the "latest release" line, and the "last push to any branch" line. Live: the archived banner, the Releases box, and the front page. | `archived: no`, AND at least one of: last push to any branch within 90 days of the capture date, or latest release dated within 12 months of the capture date. A repo with no published releases still passes when pushes are fresh; star count alone is never evidence of use. | required |
| scope-fits-newcomer | The issue body and labels line, the comment thread, and the linked-PR list in repo facts. Live: the same surfaces on the issue page. | The issue asks for one bounded piece of work a newcomer can finish: a single bug with stated wrong/right behavior, a docs change naming its files, or a small feature with maintainer-endorsed direction or acceptance criteria. Grade the size of the work, not the polish of the writeup: a terse body still passes when the ask is one bounded change (especially with a maintainer opener or a newcomer label), and enumerating parts of one task — a checklist, several files/sections in one docs change, a bug's candidate causes or suggested approaches — still counts as one piece of work, however long the list of named edits is. FAIL when any of these hold: (a) umbrella or ongoing work — the issue is explicitly a tracker (a list of other issues, by number, meant to be picked up as separate work items or separate PRs), or an open-ended "add X across the codebase" call with no defined unit of work, typically with many already-merged PRs attached; (b) unsettled design — the thread still debates the approach with no maintainer-approved direction, or an opener-only feature request has zero maintainer engagement and an unresolved product decision inside (asset "TBD", undecided UX); (c) repeated abandonment — 2 or more closed-unmerged linked PRs, or a years-old issue with multiple lapsed claims; (d) it is a support or usage question; (e) a maintainer states the fix touches core internals; (f) the opener is a `*[bot]` account and no maintainer has engaged in the thread. | required |
| nobody-on-it | Repo-facts "this issue:" line (assignees, linked PRs with per-PR state) plus the comment thread for claim comments and their dates. Live: the Assignees and Development boxes plus the thread; when the sidebar and the thread disagree, believe the thread. | All of: no assignee; no linked or thread-referenced pull request still open (in this repo or a fork); no live claim — a "I'll take this" / "working on this" / bot-claim comment dated within 180 days of the capture date whose author was not later unassigned and did not withdraw; and no maintainer comment reserving the issue for someone else. Claim comments older than 180 days with no visible follow-up are stale and do not block. Closed or merged PRs alone do not block. | required |
| contribution-policy | Repo-facts "contribution policy" line. Live: CONTRIBUTING.md (repo root or .github/) and contributor docs it links to, dedicated files such as AI_POLICY.md / AI_USAGE_POLICY.md / AGENTS.md, and PR/issue templates. | Fails only on an outright ban: the stated policy says AI-generated or AI-assisted code or documentation is not accepted, with no allowed-use carve-out. Conditions pass — disclosure requirements, "personally understand / test / be able to explain every change", human review, or closing only suspected unreviewed AI PRs. Silence also passes: no CONTRIBUTING.md, or a policy with no statement on AI or contribution tooling. | required |
| maintainer-responsive | Repo-facts "maintainer first-response sample" plus author_association badges in this issue's thread. Live: a few recently updated issues (sort by recently updated) and badges on this thread. | At least one sampled issue records a first owner/member/collaborator reply within 30 days of being opened, or a maintainer authored this issue or commented in this thread within 90 days of the capture date. | preferred |
| newcomer-signaled | The labels line on the issue, and any maintainer comment in the thread explicitly inviting a newcomer or first-timer. | The issue carries a newcomer label ("good first issue", "help wanted", "easy", "Easy to Fix", or similar), or a maintainer in the thread explicitly invites a newcomer to take it. | preferred |

## Verdict rule

Grade every check `pass`, `fail`, or `unclear`, with a one-line evidence
quote for each. `unclear` means the evidence the check names is
genuinely absent from the bundle — not merely inconvenient to find —
and on a required check it counts as a fail.

The verdict is **accept** when every `required` check passes, and
**reject** when any required check fails or comes back unclear.
`preferred` checks never change the verdict: they are still graded and
reported, and on an accepted issue they are the reasons to prefer it
over other accepted candidates.
